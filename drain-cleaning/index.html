Emergency Drain Cleaning in Stow, OH

Drain cleaning in Stow, OH starts with one question: is the clog in a single fixture or in the line every fixture shares? A slow bathroom sink usually means a blockage in its own trap, and a cable machine clears it. When several drains gurgle together, the blockage sits farther down. Drain cleaning means removing a blockage from a pipe so water flows again. Stow Pro Plumbing runs a 24/7 emergency line.

While you wait, stop sending water down the affected drain. Skip bottled drain openers, because they sit on top of a standing clog, can burn skin, and leave the plumber working in caustic water. A cup plunger is a fair first try on a sink or tub. If water is rising in a floor drain or the lowest tub in the house, that points at the main line, so read the notes on sewer line cleaning below.

Decision guide

Pick the right tool before the machine comes off the truck

Most clogs need a plunger, a hand auger or a cable machine. A smaller number need a jetter or a camera first. Choosing the wrong tool wastes a visit, and the clog comes back within weeks.

The table below shows how a plumber matches the symptom to the tool. If you are unsure where your problem fits, describe it to the dispatcher and a plumber will sort it out on site.

A cable machine is enough when

  • Only one fixture is slow or stopped, and the others drain normally.
  • The clog is hair, soap, a wad of paper or food in a 1.5 inch to 2 inch branch line.
  • A plunger moved the water a little but the drain closed up again.
  • The pipe is in decent shape and the blockage is within reach of a cable, usually under 50 feet.

Step up to jetting or a main-line call when

  • Two or more fixtures back up together, or the basement floor drain gurgles.
  • A cable opens the drain but it clogs again with grease, scale or silt. See hydro jetting for that case.
  • Roots are suspected, which is common where old clay joints sit near a tree.
  • Water rises in the lowest drain whenever the washer or dishwasher runs.

Fixture drains

Clogged sink, tub and shower drains in Stow homes

A bathroom sink drain collects toothpaste, hair and a film of soap that thickens inside the P-trap. A tub or shower drain collects far more hair, and the soap hardens around it into a gray plug a few inches below the strainer. Water that drains slowly for weeks and then stops is the classic pattern.

The quick check is to run the other fixtures. If the kitchen and the laundry drain fine, the problem belongs to that one branch and the fix is local and fast.

Slow drain that gurgles

Air is being pulled past a partial blockage. The clog is usually close to the trap, and a cable often clears it on the first pass.

Water pools around your feet

Standing water in a shower pan means the tub trap or the line just past it is blocked, most often by hair wrapped in soap.

Bad smell from one drain

A film of grease or soap has built up on the pipe wall and is decaying. Snaking removes the source, and a smell alone does not mean a sewer problem.

Two fixtures act up together

A sink and a tub that share a branch line can point to a clog downstream of both. That is where a longer cable or a camera helps.

Kitchen drains

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food and the disposal

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking fat goes down the drain warm and liquid, then cools into a waxy coating that narrows the pipe. Rice, pasta, coffee grounds and potato peels ride along and stick to that coating until the pipe is half closed.

A disposal does not fix this. It chops food into smaller pieces, but the pieces still land on the same greasy pipe wall. If your sink has a disposal and the standing water will not move, check the disposal first (see garbage disposal repair), then the trap arm behind it.

A county fact that matters for kitchen grease

Summit County's sewer backup page says backups caused by non-flushable items are not a justifiable claim. A plugged kitchen line is usually private, so the realistic path is a plumber, not a county claim. Keep grease in a can, wipe pans before washing, and the line stays open far longer.

Emergency clogs

Urgent drain trouble: what to do first when water will not go down

Call right away when sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, when a toilet and a tub both fail at once, or when water is heading for finished flooring. Drain emergencies are common after heavy rain and after a holiday dinner with a full house.

The emergency line is open 24/7. A dispatcher can arrange a same-day appointment when one is available, or send a plumber out after hours for the urgent ones.

1

Stop adding water

No showers, no laundry, no dishwasher cycle. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and in a blocked line it goes onto your floor.

2

Open a path to the cleanout

Move boxes away from the main cleanout, a capped pipe in the basement or utility room. The plumber can reach the line faster if it is clear.

3

Protect the floor and your skin

Lay towels along the wet edge. Use gloves if the water looks gray or smells like sewage, and keep children and pets out of it.

4

Look at the other drains

Note which fixtures are slow, gurgling or stopped. Tell the dispatcher, because that pattern tells the plumber whether to bring a small cable or a large one.

How the visit works

Drain snaking with a cable machine, step by step

Snaking, also called cabling, feeds a spinning steel cable into the pipe until it hits the clog. It is the right tool for hair, paper, food and soft grease, and it is quick when the clog is close to the fixture.

1

Find the access point

The plumber removes the trap, uses a cleanout, or goes in through a tub overflow or vent so the cable has a straight path.

2

Size the cable to the pipe

A narrow cable suits a bathroom sink. A heavier cable with a cutting head suits a kitchen or main branch line.

3

Break up the clog

The machine spins the cable while the plumber feeds it in. When the cable stops biting, the clog is either cleared or has wrapped around the head.

4

Flush and test

Water runs through the line for several minutes. If it drains fast, the job is done. If it still lags, the plumber explains whether jetting or a camera is the next move.

Cable machines have limits. A cable cuts a hole through a grease plug but leaves the wall coating, which is why a kitchen line can clog again. A cable can also scratch old cast iron or snag on a broken clay joint, so a plumber stops and reassesses if the cable behaves oddly.

Two smaller problems

Shower and tub drain clogs, and drain line leaks

Shower and tub drains

Tub drains hide the clog behind a strainer, a stopper linkage or a pop-up. Pull the stopper and clear what you can reach before you call. If the tub still holds water after that, the blockage is in the trap or the branch, and a small drum machine is the tool.

Drain line leaks

A drain line can leak at a slip joint under a sink, at a cracked tub trap, or in a buried section where a joint has shifted. Look for a stain on the cabinet floor, a ceiling spot under a bathroom, or soggy ground near the foundation.

A leak only shows while water runs, so a camera or a dye test helps trace it. If the leak is in a buried sewer lateral, the next stop is sewer line repair.

Local causes

What is different about drains in Stow

Medium-hard water

The city's 2024 water quality report gives about 7 grains per gallon on average. Over many years that hardness can contribute to mineral scale inside pipes, which narrows them and gives grease and hair something to catch on.

Mixed pipe ages

Older Census data puts the median Stow home at 1980, with roughly 18 percent built before 1960. Homes from the 1960s and 70s often have cast iron or clay drains, which scale and shift. Newer homes are mostly PVC.

Private lateral, county main

The City of Stow does not run sanitary sewer. Summit County does, at 330-926-2400. A blockage in your own lateral is usually a plumber call first, and a plumber can tell whether it is private or in the county main.

Wet-weather loads

Stow's flood history, including the July 2003 storms, is why sump pumps and drainage matter in low basements. A backup that shows up only in heavy rain can point to the lateral or to water entering the wrong line.

Storm drains and yard drains

Yard drains, catch basins and downspout lines fill with leaves, grit and root fibers, and they clog in spring and fall. A cable or a jetter clears them, and the camera checks for a broken tile or a low spot. This work is separate from your sanitary drains.

One rule is firm. Stow Codified Ordinance 925.01 prohibits connecting roof drains, downspouts or other storm water conveyances to the sanitary sewer. If a yard drain or downspout was ever tied into the house sewer, ask the City of Stow Building Department before any change, and the plumber can tell you what the camera shows.
