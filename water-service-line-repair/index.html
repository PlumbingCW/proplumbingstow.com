Emergency Water Line Repair in Stow, OH

Water line repair in Stow, OH covers the underground pipe that carries city water from the meter to the inside of your house. When that service line cracks, corrodes or gets pulled apart by shifting soil, you may see a wet lawn, hear nothing but a dribble at the tap, or find your meter turning with every fixture off. Urgent water line trouble: when to call right now

Treat the situation as urgent when water is rising in the yard or against the foundation, when the house has lost water entirely, or when a hole or sinkhole forms along the line path. A service line runs under pressure all day, so even a small break can move a lot of water before anyone notices.

Watch your own side of the line while waiting. The steps below keep the damage small and give the dispatcher what a plumber needs to plan the job.

Do this while you wait

Close the main shut-off valve inside the house, then open the lowest tap to relieve pressure. Keep people and pets away from any soft or sinking ground near the line. Stay out of a flooded basement if water is near the panel or outlets, and tell the dispatcher about it. If water is bubbling in the street or at the meter pit, that is the city's side, so phone the water main break line at 330-689-5700.

Signs your water service line is leaking

A wet or sunken patch of lawn

Soggy ground or a dip between the street and house during dry weather suggests water is escaping from the line.

Meter spins with taps closed

If the dial keeps turning after every fixture is shut, the water is going somewhere, and the line is a prime suspect.

Pressure that fell for no reason

A gradual drop at every faucet can follow a leak before the first puddle shows.

Muddy or rusty water

Dirt drawn into a cracked line, or corrosion stirred up by changing pressure, can discolor the supply.

Bills that climb with no change at home

A steady rise in usage on a household with the same habits points to hidden loss.

Water in the basement near the entry point

Dampness where the pipe comes through the foundation wall can be a leak right at the entry.

Where the city ends and the homeowner begins

Stow buys its drinking water from the City of Akron, and the city's water department handles the system up to your meter. The water main break emergency number is 330-689-5700, and the Water Department office, open Monday to Friday from 8:00 to 4:30, is 330-689-2889. Call the right one first so the right crew shows up.

As a general practice, a leak in the street or at the meter is the city's, and a leak on the house side of the meter is the homeowner's. Because the exact boundary can vary, ask the Water Department where its responsibility ends before you pay for digging. The water billing page on the city's site also links a Service Line Inventory document, which is worth opening if you want to see what the city lists for your property.

Underground leaks

Finding a leak on a buried line

Water that leaves a buried pipe does not always surface. Soil absorbs it, drains carry it off, or it drops into gravel under the driveway. A wet spot may sit ten or twenty feet from the break, which is why digging at the damp spot alone often misses.

Start with the meter. With the house quiet, a moving indicator means loss somewhere downstream, and closing the main valve inside separates a leak in the house from one between meter and foundation. The step-by-step version is on our leak detection page.

Why a short dig is smarter than a long one

A good locate narrows the break to a few feet, so the excavation is a small hole instead of a trench across the lawn. If the pipe is old and brittle, though, the hole can reveal more trouble than expected, and a plumber should say so before sealing it up.

Low water pressure in a Stow house

Low pressure has several causes, and the service line is only one. A partly closed valve, a scaled-up fixture, a failing pressure regulator or an old galvanized pipe that has narrowed inside can all do it. Moderately hard water, about 7 grains per gallon on average in the city's 2024 report, leaves scale in aerators, showerheads and valves, so check those first at the fixture that complains.

If every fixture is weak, look at the whole house. The city flushes and maintains roughly 2,250 fire hydrants, and the city says that work can temporarily change pressure, so a brief dip or cloudy water after flushing is not always a plumbing fault. When low pressure lasts, a plumber tests it at an outdoor spigot to see whether the line, a regulator or the house piping is at fault.

Repair or replace the service line

Spot repair is reasonable when

  • The leak is a single failed fitting or crack
  • The pipe on either side is in good condition
  • The break is close to the surface or accessible
  • The line is relatively new

Replace the line when

  • There have been repeated leaks on the same line
  • The pipe is corroded, brittle or an older material
  • Pressure has fallen along with other symptoms
  • A repair would need a long trench anyway

Replacement can be done by digging a trench or, in some cases, by pulling new pipe through the old path with less surface disruption. Which works depends on depth, what is above the line (driveways, walks, trees) and the condition of the old pipe, so the plumber decides on site. If the rest of the house plumbing is also tired, it may be worth reading about repiping at the same time.
