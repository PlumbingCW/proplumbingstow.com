Repair the old sump pump or put in a new one?

Most sump pump calls come down to this choice. A pump that fails once because of a stuck float or a tripped breaker may only need a small fix. A pump that is old, noisy and cycling nonstop is usually near the end of its life, and a replacement saves a second storm-night call.

Repair makes sense when

  • The float is stuck or tangled and the motor runs
  • The check valve has failed but the pump is sound
  • The breaker tripped and the circuit tests fine
  • The pump is fairly new and the pit is clean

Replace when

  • The motor hums but the impeller will not spin
  • The pump runs constantly or fails repeatedly
  • The housing is rusted, cracked or noisy
  • The pit is too small or sits in silt and debris

Running constantly

A pump that never shuts off may be fighting a stuck float, a very high water table or a pipe leak feeding the pit.

Not running at all

Silence while the pit fills points to a tripped breaker, a failed switch or a burned-out motor.

Strange noise

Grinding, rattling or a loud hum means worn bearings or a stone or debris in the impeller.

Tripped breaker

A breaker that keeps tripping can mean a shorted motor or a wet cord. Do not reset it over and over.

Float stuck on the wall

A float snagged on the pit side or its own cord never lifts to start the pump.

Rust or an old tag

A rusty housing or a pump that predates a past storm is on borrowed time. Plan a swap before the next wet season.

Urgent

A failed sump pump during a storm is an urgent emergency

When the pump fails in heavy rain, the pit overflows within a short time and water spreads across the basement floor. The danger is not only damp storage. Water can reach a furnace, a water heater burner, a panel or a freezer, and wet carpet and drywall take on mold quickly.

Call as soon as you see the pit filling, even if the water is still low. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and after-hours calls get the same dispatch process as a daytime one.

Safety first in a flooded basement

Do not step into standing water if the basement has live outlets, a panel or plugged-in equipment. Shut off power at the main panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it from dry ground. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside before you phone a plumber.

Battery backups, water-powered backups and new pits

A pump that depends on household power stops when the power goes out, and storms often cause both. A battery backup pump takes over when the main pump or the power fails, and a water-powered backup uses city water pressure to push water out without electricity. Each has trade-offs: batteries need testing and replacement, and water-powered units use a lot of water and need adequate pressure.

If your basement has no pit at all, or the old pit is small, cracked or full of silt, a new pit and pump can be installed. That means breaking the slab, setting a basin, running a discharge pipe and testing it. Our plumbing installation page covers how new work is planned.

Where the sump pump water goes in Stow

Stow Codified Ordinance 925.01 prohibits connecting roof drains, downspouts or other storm water conveyances from any building or land to the sanitary sewer. For a sump pump, that means the discharge goes outside to the yard or a storm connection, never to a floor drain or sanitary line. A discharge tied to the sanitary side can break the ordinance and push extra water into a system that was not built for it.

Keep the outlet several feet from the foundation and pointed away from your neighbor, so the water does not circle back into the pit. A check valve on the discharge line stops pumped water from draining back down when the pump shuts off.

Check valves and ejector pumps

The check valve is a one-way flap in the discharge pipe. If it fails, you hear a thump and gurgle after each cycle, and the pump restarts again and again. It is one of the cheaper parts to replace, and often the whole cause of a pump that seems to run forever.

An ejector pump is different. It handles waste from a basement bathroom, laundry or floor drain and lifts it to the sewer line, in a sealed pit. If a sewage pump has failed, wastewater can back up, so treat it as a more urgent and unpleasant call.
