Local conditions

What is different about water heaters in Stow homes

Stow buys its water from the City of Akron, which treats Upper Cuyahoga River water at the Lake Rockwell plant. The city's 2024 water quality report lists hardness at about 7 grains per gallon on average. That is moderately hard water, and it leaves scale on heating elements and the bottom of a tank, so sediment problems are common here and a yearly flush pays for itself.

Scale on elements and tank floors

Moderately hard water bakes a crust onto electric elements and burner chambers. The crust makes the unit work harder and can shorten element life, which is why we check for it on every no-hot-water visit.

Homes from the 1960s to the 1990s

Older Census data puts the median Stow home at 1980. Heaters in houses of that age often sit in basements beside a floor drain, with old shutoff valves that stick or weep when turned. Turn yours once now and then so you know it works.

Hydrant flushing and cloudy water

The city flushes hydrants and says this can temporarily change water pressure. Brief cloudiness or a pressure dip afterward is not always a heater fault, so run a cold tap for a minute before you blame the tank.

Cold winters

Northeast Ohio winters send very cold water into the heater, so weak recovery shows up in January first. A unit that barely keeps up in winter often has scale or a tired element, not a bad design.

Most common call

Leaking water heater: fitting, relief valve or failed tank

Where the water appears tells most of the story. A wet inlet or outlet connection on top is usually a fitting that can be tightened or replaced. A steady drip from the temperature and pressure relief valve discharge pipe means the valve is opening because of pressure or heat, and that has its own causes.

Drip from the relief valve pipe

The copper or CPVC pipe beside the tank is dripping. Excess pressure, a failed valve or a missing expansion tank can all do this.

Wet fittings on top

Water runs down the shell from the hot or cold connection. These are often repairable with new fittings or a dielectric union.

Drain valve weeping

A slow drip at the low drain valve near the floor. Sediment can hold the valve open, and a new valve often fixes it.

Rust streaks or water at the base

Water is coming from the tank bottom or seams, and the shell is stained orange or brown. This points to a failed tank.

Puddle with a gas unit

Condensation from a high-efficiency vent or a leaking drain can look like a tank leak. A plumber can tell the two apart in minutes.

Hot, damp floor near the heater

A leak on the hot outlet pipe or a manifold nearby. The heater may be fine and the pipe is the problem.

When a leaking tank means replacement

If the water is coming from inside the tank jacket, the steel liner has corroded through. No patch or sealant holds a pressurized tank, and a heater with this failure is not repairable. A plumber will confirm the source, then walk you through replacing the failed tank. Ask about the age on the data plate first, because a tank at the end of its life can fail again soon after a fitting repair.

Emergency water heater leak: what to do while you wait

A leaking tank holds 40 to 50 gallons in a typical home, and a failed seam can dump much of it onto the floor in under an hour. That is an urgent situation for carpet, drywall and anything stored on the basement floor. Call at any hour, since the dispatcher line runs 24/7 and same-day visits are offered when a plumber is open.

Gas smell or burning odor

If you smell gas near a gas water heater, leave the building at once. Call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside, and call the plumber only after you are safe.

1

Shut the cold supply

Turn the cold water valve above the heater clockwise until it stops. This cuts the flow feeding the leak.

2

Cut gas or power

Set a gas control to OFF, or switch off the dedicated breaker for an electric unit. Never leave elements on in an empty tank.

3

Move belongings and stay dry

Lift boxes, carpet and cords off the floor. Stay away from standing water near outlets, and watch for scalding if hot water is spraying.

4

Open a hot tap upstairs

Opening a faucet relieves pressure and lets the tank drain slowly through its valve if you attach a hose to a floor drain.

Plumbers carry a multimeter, a combustion analyzer on gas units, a flame sensor cleaning tool and a flush hose. Many repairs use stocked parts such as thermostats, elements, thermocouples and relief valves. If the part is on the truck, the repair can often happen on the first visit.

Repair makes sense when

  • The leak is at a fitting, drain valve or relief valve
  • The tank is under about ten years old and sound inside
  • A thermostat, element or thermocouple has failed
  • The warranty still covers the tank or parts
  • The repair costs well under the value of the remaining years

Replace when

  • Water is seeping from the tank body or seams
  • The tank is at or past its expected life and rusting
  • Sediment is thick and the burner or lower element has failed
  • Repairs have repeated within the past year
  • The unit cannot be vented or repaired to current standards
Gas and electric

No hot water or only lukewarm water

On a gas heater, the usual suspects are a pilot that went out, a dirty or weak thermocouple, a stuck gas control valve or a blocked vent. Look through the small sight glass at the base. If the pilot will not stay lit after a careful relight following the label, stop and call.

On an electric heater, check the breaker first, then the red reset button on the upper thermostat. Two elements heat the tank, and when the lower one fails you get a short supply of hot water that runs out fast. A failed upper thermostat gives no hot water at all.

Lukewarm water that fades fast

A cracked dip tube sends cold supply straight to the outlet and mixes it with hot water, so showers turn cool after a few minutes. Scale on the lower element or burner causes the same symptom. A plumber can test both, and a tank flush is often part of the fix.

Rumbling, popping or noisy tanks

Rumbling means water is trapped under a layer of sediment and boiling in pockets. With about 7 grains per gallon in Stow's water per the city's 2024 report, minerals settle on the tank floor over time. That layer insulates the water from the burner, wastes fuel and can overheat the steel.

A flush with a garden hose clears light buildup, and the best time is before the noise starts. Heavy, hardened sediment may not drain at all. If the drain valve clogs and the noise continues, the tank may be near its end, and a plumber can judge whether it is worth keeping.

Error codes and flashing lights

Modern gas heaters and tankless units show a code or a blinking light. The meaning varies by brand, so the label inside the access panel or the owner's manual is the first stop. Ignition failures, flame sensing problems, vent pressure switch trips and temperature sensor faults are the groups most codes fall into.

Write down the model, the code and when it started. That helps the dispatcher bring the right sensor or igniter. For tankless units, scale on the heat exchanger is a frequent cause of codes, and our page on tankless service and descaling covers that.

Anode rods: the cheap part that protects the tank

An anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod inside the tank that corrodes so the steel liner does not. In moderately hard water it can wear down faster than in softer supplies. Replacing it before it is spent can add years to a sound tank.

The rod sits under a hex head on top of the heater, and it needs clearance above to pull out. A plumber can inspect it and tell you whether the tank is worth saving. If the tank is already leaking, a new rod will not help.

Gas, electric or heat pump: what repair looks like for each

Gas units have more parts that can fail, such as the pilot assembly, gas valve, vent and burner, and they carry the combustion safety risk. Electric tanks are simpler. They mostly need elements, thermostats and a good power supply, and a plumber with a meter can find the fault quickly.

Hybrid heat pump heaters add a compressor, fan and condensate drain on top of the electric elements. They need free air around them and a drain for condensate. Some repairs, such as sealed refrigerant work, may call for a manufacturer service contact. Ask the dispatcher whether the plumber works on your brand before the visit.
