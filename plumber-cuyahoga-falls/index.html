Winter

Frozen and split pipes in a Cuyahoga Falls house built before 1960

Older Census data (2012 to 2016) puts the median home in Cuyahoga Falls at 1959, and about 52 percent were built in 1959 or earlier. It is the oldest housing stock of the five towns we serve. Homes from that era often have galvanized supply lines, which narrow inside as they rust and give way at joints and fittings when the cold hits.

Northeast Ohio winters swing between freeze and thaw, and a pipe in an exterior wall, a crawl space or an unheated porch is the usual victim. If a faucet drips to nothing in January, treat it as a possible freeze. Keep the heat on and call the 24/7 line before the ice turns into a split.

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Cuyahoga Falls

1

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water shutoff clockwise until it stops. In a house from the 1950s it may be an old gate valve that sticks, so turn it slowly and never force it.

2

Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, the panel or a basement sump cord, stay out of the wet area. Switch power off from a dry spot only, and leave the panel alone if you cannot reach it dry.

3

Clear the area

Move boxes, rugs and electronics off a basement floor or away from a wet wall. Old plaster and lath soak up water fast, so every minute of moving things helps.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call (855) 575-2890 and give the address, the ZIP and what you see. Say if the leak is inside a wall or under the floor, because it changes the tools the plumber brings.

Who owns the leak matters too. If water is coming up in the street or at the city side of the connection, it belongs to the city. If it is on the house side, it is yours. Ask the Cuyahoga Falls Water Department where its responsibility ends. For main breaks the city lists 330-971-8005 on weekdays from 7:30 am to 4 pm, and after hours the police non-emergency line, 330-928-2181.

Spring

Thaw, heavy rain and backed-up drains

The city keeps its sanitary and storm sewers as two separate systems. About 152 miles of sanitary sewer carry household waste to the Akron Water Pollution Control Facility. Sump pump and downspout water should go to the storm side or the yard, never into the sanitary drain.

Spring is when that rule gets tested. A basement floor drain that gurgles, a toilet that rises when the washer drains or a slow main line often points to a clog in the house sewer. A drain cleaning visit with a camera can show whether the problem is roots, a sagging section or a cast iron pipe flaking inside. Our sewer line cleaning page explains the tools.

Summer

Water heaters, scale and the softener question

The city's 2025 water quality report gives finished water a hardness of 100 to 130 ppm, which it describes as a blend of softened and unsoftened water. About two-thirds of the filtered water is softened by ion exchange before blending. That means scale builds on heating elements and tank bottoms more slowly than in harder-water towns.

If your tank water heater is rumbling, leaking at the base or running out of hot water, water heater repair is the first call. Before buying a home softener, read the city's current report on its Water Department page. Partly softened water may not need one.

Fall

Service lines, repipes and getting ready before the first freeze

Autumn is the season to look at the pipe between the street and your house. The city publishes a service line material inventory you can search by address. Lines that are lead, unknown or galvanized downstream of lead get annual notices. The city says EPA requires non-copper lines to be replaced within 10 years and that a replacement program is in development.

Look up your address before any water service line repair or repipe, and do not assume a result for a house you have not checked. If the supply lines inside are galvanized and pressure has faded, repiping may beat another patch, and a plumber can compare the options once they see the pipes.

Downtown and older buildings

Cuyahoga Falls has a Downtown Historic District with design guidelines. Older buildings there often mean older piping, and exterior changes may need design review. Ask the city before any exterior work.

On permits, the city's page lists Engineering permits for water taps, sanitary and storm sewer taps and replacing water service. Other building matters may go to the Summit County Division of Building Standards. Ask the City of Cuyahoga Falls Engineering Department or the Summit County Division of Building Standards before work starts. For a hidden pipe leak that you cannot find, see leak detection. For nearby service, we also cover Munroe Falls, which shares the same water system, and Hudson.
