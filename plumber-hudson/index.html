Housing era first

Start with the house: why age and value shape Hudson plumbing calls

Hudson had about 8,359 housing units and 8,077 households in the 2024 five-year Census estimates, and the median value of owner-occupied homes is nearly double Stow's. For a plumbing call, higher-value homes often mean bigger water heaters, several bathrooms, long pipe runs and finished spaces where a leak can hide behind tile or a ceiling.

So the first question we ask is not only what is leaking, but when the house was built and what has been remodeled since. The answer decides whether we think about old galvanized steel, cast iron drains, or newer copper and plastic lines.

Oldest homes first

The historic core: very old buildings, very old lines

David Hudson arrived in 1799, the town took his name in 1802, and his 1806 house at 318 North Main Street is described as the oldest standing structure in Summit County. Much of downtown burned in 1892, and from 1907 the village built its first water and sewer system. Downtown buildings and older houses near the center can therefore carry pipes that are many decades old.

For you, that history means two things. A recurring slow drain in an old house may be a collapsed or root-filled section of old drain line, so a camera look through sewer line repair diagnostics beats snaking it again and again. And when old supply lines start pinhole leaking, repair decisions should be made with the whole line in mind.

Rusty or brown water at the tap

Old galvanized steel corrodes from the inside. Brown water that clears slowly after running points to aging supply pipe, not only to a city main.

Low pressure in one part of the house

Mineral and rust buildup narrows old steel pipe. Pressure that drops at one bathroom but not another often means a narrowed branch line.

Gurgling drains and repeat clogs

Old cast iron and clay drains scale, sag and let roots in. A clog that returns every few months is a pipe problem, not a hair problem.

Damp plaster, stained ceilings

A stain under a bathroom in a century-old house can be a slow supply leak or a failed drain joint. Do not paint over it.

Emergency steps

Urgent leaks in older houses: what to do before the plumber arrives

1

Shut off the main water valve

Older Hudson homes may have an old gate valve that is stiff or stuck. Turn it gently and slowly, and tell the dispatcher if it will not close.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a basement panel or a finished ceiling light, switch off power from a dry spot only. If you must stand in water to reach the panel, do not.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and furniture off floors under a leak. In larger finished homes, open cabinet doors under sinks so you can see where water is coming from.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call (855) 575-2890 and give the dispatcher your address, the house age if you know it, and what you can see. A burst line may need burst pipe repair right away.

Gas smell: leave first

If you smell gas, leave the building immediately. Call 911 or the gas utility from a safe place outside, and do not use switches or phones inside. Call a plumber only after that.

Newer and bigger

Larger, higher-value homes: water heaters and remodels

A home valued near Hudson's median (nearly double Stow's) often has more than one bathroom, a finished basement and a bigger water heater, sometimes two. When one fails, the lost hot water affects the whole house, and a tank that fails in a finished space can soak drywall and flooring fast. Our water heater repair calls start with the age on the tank label and the connections around it.

Remodeled homes bring a different risk. New fixtures are sometimes tied into much older lines, so the weak point is where old pipe meets new. If a leak shows up soon after a remodel, mention the work to the dispatcher. It helps the plumber bring the right fittings.

Water source

City water, softening and what it means for your fixtures

Hudson runs its own municipal water and electric utilities, so it is not on Akron water like Stow. The city pumps groundwater from a sand-and-gravel aquifer through a well field in the western part of town. Treatment includes disinfection, fluoridation, softening and filtration, and an archived 2016 city item says the plant removes iron and manganese with zeolite softeners.

Because the city softens its water, a home softener may be less necessary than in towns with harder water. Before you buy one, ask Hudson Public Works for the current water quality report. The office is at 1140 Terex Rd, Hudson, 330-342-1750. A leak in the street is a city matter. Ask Public Works where its responsibility ends and yours begins.

Drains

Sewer or septic: know which one before a drain backs up

Not every Hudson home is on the city water, and the sewer question matters even more. Summit Public Health says about 33,000 homes in Summit County have septic systems, and Ohio's 2015 rules require every home septic system to hold an operation permit with routine maintenance. We do not know how many Hudson homes are on septic, so check yours.

Summit County Council Resolution 2025-214 also amends a 1987 sewer agreement with the Northeast Ohio Regional Sewer District to change Hudson's sanitary sewer service area. Hudson approved it as Resolution 25-86 on June 3, 2025. So ask the city or Summit Public Health whether your property is on public sewer or septic before a backup happens. The answer changes whether the problem is a house line, a tank or a main. For a plain clog, drain cleaning comes first.

Paperwork

How we handle permits and proof

Hudson takes permit applications through an online permit portal. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the city before work starts, and ask the dispatcher what the plumber will need. A plumber states a clear price before work starts, so you know what a repair covers before it begins.
