Do you need a softener in Stow? A straight answer

Stow buys its water from the City of Akron, and the Lake Rockwell plant treats it. The city's 2024 water quality report gives hardness at about 7 grains per gallon on average, which is moderately hard. That level leaves scale, but it is not extreme, so a softener is a comfort and equipment-protection choice and not a necessity.

A softener is worth considering when

  • You see white crust on faucets, showerheads and kettles
  • Your water heater, especially a tankless unit, is scaling quickly
  • You want softer-feeling water for bathing and laundry
  • You are willing to buy salt and maintain the system

You can skip it when

  • Occasional scale is easy to live with
  • A yearly water heater flush covers your equipment needs
  • You have a septic system and discharge is a concern
  • You do not want the salt, upkeep and a drain connection

If the goal is protecting a tankless heater, compare the cost of a softener with a yearly descale on the tankless water heater page. For a tank unit, the answers in water heater repair on sediment and anode rods show what scale does to the equipment.

How a softener works

Resin bed

Tiny plastic beads in the main tank hold sodium. As hard water passes, calcium and magnesium swap places with the sodium.

Regeneration

When the beads fill up, the control valve flushes them with salty brine from the brine tank, then rinses the tank.

Brine tank

A plastic tank holds salt and water. Check the salt level, and break up any crust or bridge that forms.

Control valve and drain

The valve sets the schedule and sends the used brine to a drain. It needs a power outlet for the timer or meter.

Sizing and installing a softener

Capacity is rated in grains. A rough method multiplies household size by daily water use by your hardness. With about 7 grains per gallon (per the city's 2024 report), a mid-size capacity often covers a family on a weekly regeneration, though a plumber should size it for your actual home. An oversized unit wastes salt, and an undersized unit regenerates too often.

  • 1Location: near the main water entry, with floor space for the tank and salt storage.
  • 2Bypass valve: lets you isolate the softener for service or send untreated water to outdoor taps.
  • 3Drain: a standpipe, laundry tub or floor drain with an air gap for the backwash line.
  • 4Electrical: a grounded 120 V outlet within reach of the valve's transformer.
  • 5Plumbing layout: soft water to the water heater, but outdoor faucets and irrigation usually stay untreated.

Discharge: keep it out of storm lines

Used brine belongs in an indirect drain that connects to your home's waste system, with an air gap. Do not tie it into a sump line, a downspout or a storm drain. Stow Codified Ordinance 925.01 prohibits connecting roof drains, downspouts and other storm water conveyances to the sanitary sewer, so keep the water paths separate. If your home is on septic, ask Summit Public Health and your installer first.

Softener and conditioner repair

Common faults include a failed control valve or timer, a stuck brine valve, a clogged injector, a salt bridge in the brine tank and a bad riser tube or O-ring. Resin beads wear out over many years, especially when chlorine and iron are present, and fouled resin can sometimes be cleaned. A cracked tank after a move is another cause, since tanks can be dropped in transit.

Tell the dispatcher the brand and model, the age of the unit and what it is doing, such as running out of salt, not regenerating or leaving hard water. If the system overflows from the brine tank or drain, switch the unit to bypass and call.

Brine tank cleaning

Every few years, empty the brine tank and wash out the sludge at the bottom. A tank with caked salt, a mushy bottom or a bridge of hardened salt blocks brine from reaching the resin. A plumber can clean it, check the brine well and check the float or safety valve.
