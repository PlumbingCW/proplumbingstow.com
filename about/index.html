How we work

What happens when you call

The dispatcher asks four things: your address, what you see, whether water or sewage is still flowing, and how to reach you. That is enough to decide whether the call is an emergency and which kind of plumber you need.

A plumber then looks at the problem in person and explains the repair and its price before starting. If the price or the plan does not suit you, you can say no before work begins. We do not take payment details over the dispatch line.

What we promise and what we do not

We promise a clear price before work starts and honest advice about whether a repair or a replacement makes more sense. We do not promise a specific arrival time, because traffic, weather and the day's emergencies decide that. We also do not publish reviews or ratings on this site, because we would rather you check licensing and ask questions than trust a number.

Checking a plumber's license in Ohio

Ohio licenses commercial plumbing contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, and the state's eLicense lookup lets you check a license by name. Residential plumbing contractors are not at the state level, so the local building department decides registration. In Stow, ask the City of Stow Building Department at 330-689-2729.

Our dispatcher can tell you who is coming and answer questions about the work. Ask before the visit, and ask for the same answers in writing if you prefer.

Why local detail matters

Plumbing problems depend on where the house sits. Stow's water comes from Akron's Lake Rockwell plant and averages about 7 grains per gallon according to the city's 2024 report, so scale builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Sewer service comes from Summit County, not the city, which matters when a drain backs up. Kent sits in Portage County and handles permits through its own building division.

That is why each town page on this site explains its own water, sewer and permit rules. Start with Cuyahoga Falls or Kent, or see all service areas.

How pricing works

A plumber cannot price a problem nobody has seen, so we do not quote repairs over the phone. What decides the price is what failed, how hard it is to reach, which parts it needs and whether the visit is a scheduled one or a 3 a.m. emergency. After-hours visits can cost more than daytime ones, so ask the dispatcher about that when you call.

The plumber looks first, then states the repair and its price. Nothing starts until you say yes.

What to expect at the visit

1

Stay reachable

Keep your phone nearby after you call, and have someone home to let the plumber in.

2

A look at the problem

They check the fixture, pipe or appliance, and the shutoff valves, before touching anything.

3

A plain explanation

You hear what failed, what the repair options are and what each one costs.

4

The repair and a check

After the work, the plumber tests it with you and tells you whether a permit or inspection applies.

What we do not do

Plumbing dispatch covers pipes, drains, fixtures, water heaters, sewer lines, sump pumps and related work. Foundation repair, structural waterproofing, electrical repair and burner service on heating systems may need a different trade. The dispatcher will say so rather than book the wrong visit.
