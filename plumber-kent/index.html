What is going wrong in your Kent home or rental right now

Most Kent calls fall into three piles: water where it should not be, a drain or sewer line that will not move, and no hot water. Each pile has a different first move, and each one has a different answer to the question of who is responsible. That second question is where Kent differs most from Stow.

Below you will find the quick actions, the signs that point to a lateral problem, a side-by-side table of Kent and Stow rules, and the questions Kent callers ask us most. Emergency dispatch for Kent runs around the clock, including nights and weekends.

Portage County, not Summit

Kent has its own state-certified Building Division at 930 Overholt Rd, 330-678-8107. Permit questions for work in Kent go there, not to the City of Stow.

Groundwater supply

Kent's city water comes from the Breakneck Creek Wellfield in the Buried Valley Aquifer plus a rock well. It is not bought from Akron, so scale and water-chemistry habits from Stow do not transfer one to one.

A rental-heavy town

About 59 percent of Kent homes are rented, based on the Census owner-occupied rate of 40.8 percent (2020 to 2024). Repeat clogs and water heater failures often involve both a tenant and a landlord.

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Kent

1

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water shutoff clockwise until it stops. In an older rental house or duplex it may be a gate valve that sticks, so turn it slowly and do not force it.

2

Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, a panel or a basement sump cord, stay out of the wet area and switch off power from a dry spot only. If you cannot reach the panel dry, leave it alone.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. In student rentals, that often means pulling belongings away from a basement wall before the water spreads.

4

Signs your drain or sewer lateral is the real problem

Several fixtures back up together

When a tub gurgles as the washer drains, or a basement floor drain bubbles after a flush, the blockage is usually past the branch lines, in the main drain or the lateral.

Slow drains that keep returning

A snaked kitchen line that clogs again in a few weeks points to grease, scale or a belly in old pipe, not just a bad day.

Wet or sunken ground in the yard

A soggy strip or a dip between the house and the street can mean a cracked or offset lateral leaking into the soil.

Sewer smell near a floor drain

A dry trap causes it sometimes. A smell that stays after you run water into the drain can mean a partial blockage or a damaged line.

Kent's older housing makes these signs more likely. Older Census data (2012 to 2016) put the median home build year at 1971, with about 47 percent built before 1970. Homes from that era often have galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains and clay laterals, all of which scale, crack or shift with age. If your block has had repeated backups, drain cleaning with a camera look is the first step before anything is dug up.

Who owns what: Kent's rules compared with Stow

Kent Codified Ordinance 915.05 says the property owner maintains, repairs and replaces the sanitary sewer lateral in the right-of-way and on private property, up to and including the wye. The city repairs the right-of-way portion only during street reconstruction. That means a Kent owner or landlord decides on the camera inspection and the repair, and tenants should hand the finding to the landlord.

Hot water failures and older downtown buildings

No hot water is the third big Kent call. Kent's treated water measures 101 mg/L of hardness (the city's 2025 report), roughly 6 grains per gallon, which still leaves scale on heating elements and tank bottoms over time. A tank that rumbles, leaks at the base or trips its pilot repeatedly is often a water heater repair job, but a leaking tank body usually means replacement.

Kent Dam, built in 1836 and restored with a bypass channel in 2005 to create Heritage Park, is a reminder that downtown sits near the river. As a general caution, low lots near the river can take on water in heavy rain, so basement floor drains and sump setups matter in older downtown buildings.

Rental tip: document before you call

Take a photo of the leak or backup and note the time. Send it to the landlord. If the problem is a lateral, the owner is responsible up to the wye, so the record helps settle the repair quickly.

Repair the lateral when

  • A camera shows one cracked joint or a single root intrusion
  • The rest of the line is straight and round
  • The clog is grease or debris that jetting clears

Replace a section when

  • The camera shows a collapsed or offset pipe
  • Roots return soon after every cleaning
  • Clay or cast iron sections are broken in several places

For a cracked or collapsed line, read our sewer line repair page.
