Hard water

Hard water, scale and the water heater

Stow buys its water from Akron, and the city's 2024 water quality report gives hardness of about 7 grains per gallon on average, within a range of 4 to 8. That is moderately hard water. Over time it leaves scale on heating elements, in the bottom of a tank, inside tankless heat exchangers and in faucet aerators.

The practical result is a water heater that works harder as scale builds. Rumbling or popping from a tank, slower recovery and weaker flow from a showerhead are all common scale symptoms. They are general signs, not a diagnosis of any one house, so a plumber still needs to look before recommending a repair.

Water pressure

Cloudy water or a pressure dip after hydrant flushing

The City of Stow maintains nearly 2,250 fire hydrants, and the city says flushing and maintenance can temporarily change water pressure. If the water looks cloudy or the pressure dips for a short time, it is not always a plumbing fault inside your home. Run a cold tap for a few minutes. If it does not clear, or if only one fixture is affected, the problem is more likely inside the house.

A water main break in the street goes to the city at 330-689-5700. A leak on the house side of the meter is the homeowner's, and the water department can tell you where its responsibility ends.

Choosing a softener

Is a water softener worth asking about?

A softener is a choice, not a repair. Moderately hard water is livable, and some households live with it and descale fixtures instead. If scale is wearing out heaters or clouding glass, ask about softener and filtration options and what installation involves in your layout.

Whichever way you lean, avoid buying equipment based on a single number. The city's figure is a system-wide average, and your household's habits matter as much as the water.
