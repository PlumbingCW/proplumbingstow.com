Does Coconut Oil Clog Drains?

Yes, coconut oil can clog drains in Stow, OH. Coconut oil is a fat, and fats solidify as they cool. It stays liquid in a warm kitchen, then turns solid at cool room temperature, so once it slides into a cold pipe it hardens and coats the walls. Over weeks it builds a waxy layer that catches food scraps, hair and soap, until water barely moves.

Pouring hot water after it does not solve the problem. Hot water only carries the oil further down the line, where it cools and sets again. Scrape it into a container, let it harden and put it in the trash.

Fat chemistry

The science: why a liquid becomes a plug

Coconut oil is unusual among kitchen oils because it is made mostly of saturated fat. Saturated fats pack tightly when they cool, which is why coconut oil sits as a white solid in a jar on a cool shelf and melts in your palm. The melting point is roughly in the mid-seventies Fahrenheit, so it is solid at cool room temperature and liquid in a warm room.

Drain pipes are cooler than your kitchen. The pipe under the sink and the line running through a basement wall or slab sit at the temperature of the surrounding air and soil, which is usually well below the point where coconut oil stays liquid. So the oil does not stay liquid for long once it leaves the drain opening.

It is not only coconut oil

Bacon grease, butter, lard and shortening behave the same way. Even liquid vegetable oils can cling to pipe walls and thicken when they mix with soap and food bits. Coconut oil is just the one that sets up fastest.

Build-up

What happens inside the pipe

A thin wax coat forms

The first cooled oil sticks to the pipe wall. It looks harmless, and the drain still runs normally.

Debris joins the layer

Coffee grounds, hair, lint and soap get caught in the sticky film, and the layer thickens.

The bore narrows

Water has less room. Sinks gurgle, empty slowly and may smell sour as food rots in the film.

A full blockage

A plug of fat and debris stops the flow. It can sit in the trap, the branch drain or farther down the lateral.

The right move

What to do with leftover coconut oil

1

Let the pan cool

Wait until the oil is warm but no longer hot. Never move a pan of scalding oil to the sink.

2

Wipe or scrape

Use a paper towel or silicone spatula to push the oil and bits into a can, jar or empty carton.

3

Let it solidify

Leave the container on the counter or in the fridge until the fat sets hard.

4

Bin it

Drop the container in the household trash. Use a spoon to scoop any hardened bits into the same container.

5

Wash the pan lightly

Rinse what remains with a small amount of soap, once most of the fat is gone.

The takeaway: dish soap emulsifies grease so it can wash off your plate, but the fat is still in the water. Once the soap and heat are gone, the fat can separate again and stick to the pipe.

Season

Why winter makes fat clogs worse

Northeast Ohio has cold winters with freeze and thaw cycles, and colder pipes mean fat sets sooner and closer to the sink. A line along an outside wall or through an unheated garage cools fastest. Oil that might have traveled a few yards in July can harden within a few feet in January.

That is why many households notice slow kitchen drains in the cold months. If you cook with coconut oil often, treat winter as the time to be strictest about scraping pans, and avoid rinsing greasy pots under the tap.

Get help

When it is time to call a plumber

Signs the fat has already built up

Call if a kitchen sink drains slowly after you stopped pouring oil, if there is a greasy smell from the drain, or if the sink backs up into the dishwasher side. These signs point to a fatty layer that a plunger cannot remove.

A plumber can open the line with a cable and, for a heavy coat, scour the pipe wall with high-pressure water. If the blockage reaches the main line, the work moves from drain cleaning to sewer line cleaning, and the dispatcher on our 24/7 line can book the visit.

Fat in Stow's sewer system

Stow homes drain to the Summit County sewer, which is run by the county rather than the city. The county bills quarterly. Summit County's sewer backup page says backups caused by non-flushable items and private lateral obstructions are not a justifiable claim.

In practical terms, a fatty clog in your own lateral is usually a problem to solve through a plumber first. A plumber can tell whether the blockage is yours or in the county main. Since coconut oil hardens at cool room temperature, the cool pipes in an unheated crawl space or a basement run are where it tends to set.
