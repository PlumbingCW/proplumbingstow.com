New Garbage Disposal Installation in Stow, OH

A new garbage disposal installation in Stow, OH means mounting a grinder under the kitchen sink flange, connecting it to the drain and the dishwasher hose, and giving it safe power. A first-time install on a sink with no disposal usually takes longer than a swap, because the electrical and drain parts have to be set up from scratch. A like-for-like replacement is the quicker version of the same job.

Before you buy, check three things: the size of the cabinet under the sink, whether a switched outlet or a wired switch exists, and whether a dishwasher drains into the sink. Those answers decide most of the parts list and most of the work.

The basics

How a disposal fits under the sink

A disposal hangs from the sink drain opening. A metal flange sits in the sink hole, a mounting ring and snap ring lock the unit to it, and plumber's putty seals the flange against the sink. The grinder body then drops into place, and its discharge port connects to the trap with a short elbow pipe.

Inside, a motor spins a plate with lugs that push scraps against a grind ring. It does not use blades like a blender. Water carries the fine paste into the drain, which is why you run cold water while it works and for a few seconds after.

Motors come in rough size classes from one-third horsepower up to one horsepower. A bigger motor handles tougher scraps and runs a bit quieter, but it also weighs more and needs more room below the sink.

Choosing the right unit for your kitchen

Continuous-feed models run while the wall switch is on and you feed scraps by hand. Batch-feed models only run when a stopper lid is twisted into place, which suits households with small children. Both do the same grinding. The difference is how the unit is switched on.

Mounting style matters in a cramped cabinet. Some units use a three-bolt mount that is quick to install, while others use a more rigid mount for heavier models. A tall unit may collide with the trap or a water filter on the same cabinet floor, so measure down from the sink bottom before you order.

A prep checklist before install day

  • 1Clear the cabinet under the sink and lay down a towel or a shallow tray.
  • 2Find out whether the disposal will plug in or be wired, and look for a wall switch or a switched outlet.
  • 3Check that the shutoff valves under the sink turn. Stuck valves are common on older homes.
  • 4Note whether a dishwasher drain hose connects to the sink or to the old disposal.
  • 5Keep the manual, the mounting hardware and the power cord or wiring kit in one place.
  • 6Tell the dispatcher about any slow drain, since a clogged trap changes the plan.

Common mistakes with new disposals

The first one is forgetting the dishwasher knockout. Many units ship with a thin plug inside the dishwasher inlet. If it stays in place, the dishwasher cannot drain, and you find out when the first load leaves a pool at the bottom. The plug needs to be punched out and removed from the unit before the hose goes on.

The second is overtightening the flange. Cranking the mounting screws squeezes the putty out unevenly and can crack a thin sink or leave a slow weep. The third is a poor trap slope, which lets food paste settle in a low spot and clog the line within weeks.

What a disposal should never see

Grease is the biggest enemy. It leaves the disposal as a warm liquid, then cools and coats the drain walls farther down. Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and onion skins wrap around the grinding plate and slow it down. Coffee grounds, eggshells and starchy foods like pasta and rice pack into a dense paste in a trap.

A disposal also does not replace a trash can. If the drain is already slow, drain cleaning should come first, because grinding more food into a partly blocked line only makes the problem worse.

Stow permits, county sewer and what goes down the drain

The City of Stow's building code and permits page lists Plumbing Systems among the work that needs a building permit, and it does not mention disposals by name. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the City of Stow Building Department before work starts. Its main line is 330-689-2729.

Disposals feed the Summit County sewer system, since the City of Stow does not provide sanitary sewer service. Summit County's sewer backup page says backups caused by private lateral obstructions or non-flushable items are not a justifiable claim. Keeping grease and fibrous waste out of the line is the simplest way to avoid that problem.

What you can do and when to call a plumber

A handy homeowner can swap an existing disposal for a similar one if the shutoff valves and outlet are in good shape. That job mostly involves removing the old unit's mount, hanging the new one and reconnecting two hoses. Turn off power at the breaker before touching any wiring.

Call a plumber when a flange needs to be cut into a sink for the first time, when the drain pieces do not line up, when the cabinet shows water damage, or when you are not sure the power is safe. If the disposal you already own hums, jams or leaks, a garbage disposal repair or replacement visit is a better starting point than buying a unit and hoping it fits.
