Leaky Faucet Repair in Stow, OH

Leaky faucet repair in Stow, OH usually means replacing one worn part inside the faucet: a rubber washer, an O-ring, a seat or a cartridge. A leaky faucet is a fixture that drips from the spout or seeps around the handle because an internal seal no longer closes tight. Shut off the supply valves under the sink, take the handle apart, and swap the part that matches your faucet type.

Most homeowners with a screwdriver, an adjustable wrench and a few dollars of parts can finish this in an hour. If the faucet body is pitted, the valve seat is eaten away, or the shutoff valves under the sink will not turn, a plumber is the better call.

The cause

Why a faucet starts to drip

Every faucet has a part that presses against a seat to stop the water. Over time that part hardens, cracks or wears flat, and water slips past it. The drip is just the first sign that the seal has lost its grip.

Which part fails depends on the design. Old compression faucets, the kind with separate hot and cold handles that you crank down, use a rubber washer on the end of a stem. Single-handle faucets use a cartridge or a ball assembly. Ceramic disc faucets use two polished discs that can chip or collect grit.

Drip from the spout

A worn washer, seat or cartridge is letting water by. The drip often speeds up when you turn the handle off hard.

Water around the handle base

A dry or cracked O-ring or a loose packing nut is letting water climb the stem and seep out under the handle.

Weak or sputtering flow

Mineral scale is clogging the aerator screen at the tip of the spout. This is a cleaning job, not a leak.

Wet cabinet under the sink

The leak may be at a supply line, a shutoff valve or the drain, not the faucet. Dry everything and watch where the first bead of water forms.

The repair

Fixing a dripping faucet, step by step

1

Shut off the water

Turn the two small oval handles under the sink clockwise until snug. Open the faucet to drain the line and confirm the flow stops. Plug the drain with a rag so a screw cannot fall in.

2

Remove the handle

Pry off the decorative cap, then back out the screw with a Phillips or hex key. Lift the handle straight off. If it sticks, wiggle it gently instead of prying hard against the finish.

3

Pull the cartridge or stem

Unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip with an adjustable wrench. Take the cartridge or stem out and note how it sits, because it goes back the same way.

4

Replace the worn part

Take the old part to a hardware store and match it. Swap the washer, O-ring or whole cartridge, and wipe mineral crust off the seat with a rag and a little white vinegar.

5

Reassemble and test

Put everything back in reverse order, open the supply valves slowly, and run the faucet for a minute. Check the spout and the handle base for any bead of water.

A quick note on the aerator

If the flow is weak rather than dripping, unscrew the aerator from the spout tip by hand or with a rag and pliers. Soak it in vinegar, brush the screen and put it back. That often fixes the problem with no parts at all.

Know the limit

When to stop and call a plumber

These are plumber jobs

Call if the shutoff valves under the sink will not close, because forcing a stuck valve can snap it and flood the cabinet. Call if the faucet still drips after you fit a new cartridge, since the seat or the body is likely damaged. Call if you see green corrosion on supply lines, a soft cabinet floor, or water that keeps appearing after you dry everything.

A plumber can replace the faucet, the shutoff valves and the supply lines in one visit, and the dispatcher on our 24/7 line can book faucet repair for the same day when schedules allow.

Look wider

Hidden leaks that look like a drip

A faucet drip is easy to see. A leak inside the wall or under a slab is not. If your faucet is fine but the water bill keeps climbing, or you hear water running when everything is off, the problem may sit elsewhere in the house.

A plumber can pressure-test lines and listen for leaks with sensitive equipment. That work falls under leak detection, and it is worth booking before a small hidden leak rots a floor.
