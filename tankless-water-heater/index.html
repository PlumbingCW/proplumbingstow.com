Tankless Water Heater Service in Stow, OH

A tankless water heater in Stow, OH heats water on demand with a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger, so there is no storage tank to run out. The trade-off is that the heat exchanger scales up in moderately hard water, and that is why flushing and descaling matter. Flushing and descaling a tankless water heater

Stow's water averages about 7 grains per gallon, according to the city's 2024 water quality report. That is moderately hard, so minerals leave a scale film inside the heat exchanger every time water is heated. The film narrows the passages, reduces flow and makes the unit work harder.

A flush runs a cleaning solution through the heat exchanger. A plumber closes the isolation valves, connects hoses to the service ports and circulates white vinegar or a descaler approved by the manufacturer with a small pump for a set time. Then the unit is rinsed with clean water, and the inlet screen is cleaned. Using the wrong chemical can damage seals, so follow the manual.

How often depends on hardness and how much hot water you use. In water like Stow's, once a year is a common starting point, and a household with heavy use may want it sooner. Ask the manufacturer for its interval, and check your warranty terms, since some require documented flushing. If your unit has no service valves, the plumber can add them so future flushes are simple.

What to settle before a tankless installation

A tankless unit asks more of the house than a tank does. Gas models draw a high burst of gas, so the existing pipe may be too small. They need a sealed vent of stainless or PVC, depending on the model. Condensing models drain acidic condensate that needs a neutralizer and a drain.

  • 1Gas line size: confirm the pipe can supply the unit's full input rating at its far end.
  • 2Venting: pick a side wall or roof route and check clearances to windows and meters.
  • 3Condensate drain: plan a neutralizer and a path to a floor drain for condensing models.
  • 4Electrical: gas models need a 120 V outlet, and electric models need heavy circuits and panel room.
  • 5Recirculation: a pump and return line, or a built-in recirculation mode, shortens the wait for hot water.

For the broader planning list, see our page on planned water heater installation, which also covers tank and hybrid choices. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the City of Stow Building Department before work starts.

Error codes and cold water sandwich problems

Ignition or flame codes

The unit tries to light and shuts down. A dirty flame sensor, low gas pressure or a failing igniter are common causes.

Exhaust or vent codes

A blocked, loose or undersized vent pipe, or a failing combustion fan, triggers a safety lockout.

Flow or temperature codes

Scale on the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet screen can starve the flow sensor and cause erratic temperatures.

Cold water sandwich

A burst of hot water, a cold slug, then hot again. It comes from short, repeated draws, and a recirculation setup or buffer tank can help.

Codes vary by brand, so check your manual for the code table and note the number before you call. If a code returns after a reset, the cause is still there. A plumber can read the fault and bring the right part, often a sensor, igniter or inlet screen.

Tankless water heater leaks and repairs

Most leaks start at a connection, such as an isolation valve, a union or the condensate line. A weeping drain on a condensing unit can also be a clogged neutralizer. A cracked heat exchanger is the serious case, and it often means replacing the unit.

Turn off the water supply and the gas or power if you see water at the base, and call. A tankless unit that is leaking inside the cabinet should not run.

Gas smell near the unit

If you smell gas, leave the building. Call 911 or the gas utility from outside before you call a plumber, and do not use switches or phones inside.

Tankless versus a tank: an honest comparison

Tankless works well when

  • You can supply the gas pipe or electrical capacity
  • Hot water use is long and steady, such as several showers in a row
  • Wall space matters more than floor space
  • You will flush it every year

A tank may suit better when

  • The gas line or panel would need a costly upgrade
  • Several fixtures run in short bursts
  • You want lower upfront cost and simple repairs
  • You rarely think about maintenance

Tankless units do not store hot water, so you are not paying to keep 40 gallons warm all day. They also do not solve a hardness problem. Scale builds in them faster than in a tank, and a failed exchanger can cost more to replace than a tank.
