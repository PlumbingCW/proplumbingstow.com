When a plumbing problem in Stow is an emergency

An emergency plumber earns the name when waiting until morning makes the damage bigger. Water running through a ceiling, sewage coming up a floor drain or a tank leaking onto a finished basement floor all fall in that group. Each one gets worse by the hour, which is why our dispatch line is open 24/7.

Not every problem needs a midnight visit. A slow drip under a sink can wait for the next available appointment. The six situations below are the ones where we treat a call as an emergency.

Burst or leaking pipe

Water you cannot stop at the fixture. Shut off the main valve, then call. See emergency burst pipe repair.

Sewage backup

Gurgling toilets, water rising in a tub or sewage at a floor drain usually mean the main line is blocked. Sewer line cleaning clears it.

Water heater leaking or dead

A tank leaking onto the floor needs the cold supply shut off right away. See water heater repair.

Overflowing toilet or fixture

If the shutoff valve will not stop the flow, the water keeps coming. Toilet repair stops it at the source.

Sump pump failure in a storm

A silent pump during heavy rain can flood a basement in hours. Sump pump repair gets the pit working again.

Hidden leak or runaway meter

A meter that spins with every tap closed means water is escaping somewhere. Leak detection finds where.

Smell gas? Leave first, then call

If you smell gas, leave the building immediately. From a safe place outside, call 911 or your gas utility. Do not use switches, phones or anything electrical inside. Call a plumber only after the gas utility says the home is safe.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

1

Shut off the water

Turn the main shutoff valve clockwise, usually near where the line enters the basement or crawlspace. For one fixture, use its own valve.

2

Stop using drains

With a sewage backup, do not flush, run the dishwasher or start the washer. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

3

Cut power near water

If water is near outlets, a panel or an electric water heater, turn off those breakers only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

4

Protect and photograph

Move rugs and valuables out of the wet area. Take photos of the damage before cleanup, since your insurer may ask for them.

Local plumbing facts

What makes plumbing in Stow different

Stow sits in Summit County between Cuyahoga Falls and Kent, and a few local facts change how a plumbing problem here gets solved. Knowing them saves a phone call or two when something goes wrong.

City water from Akron

Stow buys its water from Akron, drawn from the Upper Cuyahoga River and treated at Lake Rockwell. The city's 2024 report puts hardness near 7 grains per gallon, enough to leave scale inside water heaters and on faucet aerators.

Sewer is run by the county

The City of Stow does not run sanitary sewer service. Summit County Environmental Services does, and it bills quarterly. A blocked line inside your property is a plumber's job. A problem in the county main is the county's.

Downspouts stay out of the sewer

Stow Codified Ordinance 925.01 bars roof drains and downspouts from connecting to the sanitary sewer. If a basement floods during storms, the fix is a sump pump with a proper outdoor discharge.

Inspections run on set days

Stow's Building Department holds plumbing inspections on Monday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings. Ask the department before starting work that may need a permit.

Most Stow homes date from the 1960s through the 1990s, according to older Census figures, and about 72% are owner-occupied. Homes from that stretch often have copper supply lines, cast iron or plastic drains, and in some cases polybutylene pipe from the late 1970s to mid 1990s. A plumber checking a leak near Silver Springs Park or off Darrow Road should know which era the house belongs to before choosing a repair.

Winter matters too. Northeast Ohio freeze and thaw cycles stress exposed pipes, outdoor spigots and unheated crawlspaces. If you commute along State Route 8 or Route 59 and come home to a cold house, check for frozen lines before turning faucets back on.
