If you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Tallmadge

1

Shut off the main valve

Find the main water shutoff where the line enters the house, often in the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops. In an older house the valve may be stiff, so use steady pressure instead of force.

2

Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, a panel or a basement freezer, stay out of the wet area. Switch off the breaker only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and stored items away from the leak. Keep kids and pets out of the room, because wet floors and hot water make a bad mix.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Older homes and old pipe around the Circle

Homes in Tallmadge come in many ages, and the oldest buildings cluster near the Circle. The city's own history goes back to a settlement in 1807, so some structures here have seen plenty of plumbing repairs. A house with mixed pipe, such as old steel next to newer copper or plastic, tends to leak at the joints where two materials meet.

That is why the building's age is worth mentioning on the first call. A slow drip behind a wall in an older house is a job for leak detection, which finds the wet spot before anyone opens drywall. In a newer home, the same drip is more likely a fitting or a supply line.

Stained ceiling or wall

A brown ring that grows after you run the shower points to a supply or drain leak above it.

Water meter spinning

Shut every fixture off. If the meter dial still turns, water is escaping somewhere on the house side.

Low pressure at one tap

One weak faucet often means a clogged aerator. Scale from moderately hard water is a common cause.

Musty smell in the basement

A damp, earthy odor near a wall can mean a hidden leak or seepage, not just humidity.

Low spots, park-side basements and flood-prone roads

A July 10, 2013 storm flooded East Avenue, as reported by Cleveland Scene. For a homeowner the lesson is simple: basements on low ground near flooded roads need a working sump pump and a way for water to leave the house. If yours runs often, or never ran during the last heavy rain, see our sump pump service page.

A sump that fails in a storm is an emergency, because the basement fills while the rain is still falling. Unplug anything on the floor, keep off wet carpet and call the 24/7 line.

Akron water, Tallmadge reservoirs, and what scale does to a water heater

Akron draws surface water from the Upper Cuyahoga River through three impounding reservoirs and treats it at its own plant. Tallmadge buys that water, pumps it to city reservoirs and sends it to homes. The city's 2022 water quality report lists hardness averaging about 7 grains per gallon, range 4 to 8, which is moderately hard.

Moderately hard water leaves scale on heating elements, tank bottoms and faucet aerators. A tank that rumbles or heats slowly is often scaled up. When a water heater leaks from the tank itself, repair is not an option, and a water heater replacement is the next step.

Two sewer districts, one question to ask first

Tallmadge has two sewer districts. District I sewage goes to the Akron treatment plant, and District II goes to the Fishcreek plant that Summit County's Department of Environmental Services runs. We cannot tell you from the street which district your address is in, and we will not guess.

Before a major sewer job, ask the city which district the address falls in. For a backed-up line, a camera look and sewer line cleaning come first, and a plumber can tell whether the blockage is in the house line or farther out. A stubborn kitchen or bath clog usually means a shorter, simpler line cleaning.
