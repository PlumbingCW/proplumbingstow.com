Basement Waterproofing in Stow, OH

Basement waterproofing in Stow, OH means keeping groundwater and surface water from reaching your basement floor and walls. Plumbers handle part of that job: interior drain tile, sump pits and pumps, floor drains, window wells and the downspout and grading fixes that carry rain away from the house.

Not every wet basement is a waterproofing problem, and not every one is a plumbing problem. This page sorts out which is which, so you call the right trade the first time. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and the price is clear before work starts.

Plan before you book: what to know about your basement

A few answers help the plumber a great deal. Does the water show up only during or after rain, or does it appear on dry days too? Is it a damp patch, a trickle through a wall crack, or a puddle in the middle of the floor? Does it come near the water heater, the washer or a floor drain?

Photos, a rough date when it started, and the location help. Also check your downspouts and gutters, since a downspout dumping beside the foundation causes a lot of wet basements and costs little to fix.

When water in a basement is a plumbing problem

Water on the floor in dry weather often points to a pipe. A supply line that has pinholed, a water heater tank that is weeping, a washer hose that has split, a cracked drain or a sewer line backing up can all put water on the floor. These are plumbing jobs, and our leak detection page explains how a plumber finds hidden supply leaks.

Callers often describe storm water entering a basement through a wall or floor, and it often turns out not to be plumbing at all. That is a drainage or foundation question. We would rather tell you so than sell a repair that cannot work.

Water only after rain

This usually points to groundwater, downspouts, grading or a failed sump, not a pipe.

Water on dry days

A supply line, water heater, washer line or drain leak is more likely.

Wet near a floor drain

A backed-up floor drain can mean a sewer or lateral blockage.

Damp wall with white powder

That powder is mineral residue from water passing through masonry. It shows moisture, not which pipe or crack.

What plumbers can do about a wet basement

Interior drain tile is a perforated pipe set below the floor along the wall that collects water and carries it to a sump pit. A sump pump then sends it outside. Our sump pump repair and installation page covers the pump, the pit, backups and discharge.

Floor drains can be cleared or repaired, and window wells can be cleaned and given a proper drain. Downspouts and grading fixes move roof and yard water away. Exterior excavation, wall crack injection and foundation repair belong to a waterproofing or foundation contractor.

A plumber is the right call when

  • A pipe, fixture or appliance line is leaking
  • A floor drain or sewer line is backing up
  • A sump pump or pit needs repair or replacing
  • A downspout or discharge needs rerouting

Call a foundation contractor when

  • The wall has a widening or stair-step crack
  • Water comes through the wall or floor joint in rain
  • The wall bows inward or the floor heaves
  • Exterior excavation is needed

Waterproof flooring and paint do not stop water entry

Waterproof basement flooring, sealing paint and epoxy coatings are a cosmetic layer. They can make a damp slab look better, but they do not stop water coming in. Water pushed behind a coating can blister it, lift tiles and trap moisture against the wall.

Fix the water source first, whether that is a leak, a sump, a gutter or a crack. Then finish the floor. If you see standing water or persistent dampness, our sewer line cleaning page covers a backed-up main, which can also show up as basement water.
